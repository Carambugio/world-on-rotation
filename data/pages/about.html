World on Rotation pins mixes and playlists to the places their music comes from. A capital city, a port town, a mountain village, a whole region or a whole country: spin the globe, pick a place and press play on a selection put together by someone who knows its sound.

Every mix and playlist is chosen by a person. Each selector has a page of their own, with everything they have pinned to the map. Mixes play through Mixcloud, which is licensed, so the artists and labels behind the music get paid. Playlists play through the service they were built on.

The music comes with context. Every country has a guide to its sound: the traditions and instruments, the eras that shaped it, the music its diaspora carried abroad, what is happening now, and the labels, festivals and records worth tracking down. Places get guides of their own, down to where to hear live music, where to buy records and what to tune in to.

If you would rather not choose, the turntables will choose for you: one record for each half of the world, starting with a random mix from your side of it. Every place you play adds a stamp to your own passport, kept in your browser.

Who is behind it

World on Rotation is a project of Rhythm Passport, the independent world music platform based in London, with its online magazine, London gig guide, live events and the monthly radio show Rhythm Passport Presents. It is run by Rhythm Passport’s director, Marco Canepari, an ethnomusicologist and music journalist who has spent more than 20 years in London’s world and grassroots music scene.
